# COSTCO WHOLESALE CORP /NEW (COST)

## Business model
- Low prices on a narrow assortment (under 4,000 active SKUs per core warehouse) drive high volume and fast turnover, which supports a low gross margin. [N482][N492]
- Members pay a $65 U.S. fee, recognized ratably. Executive members earn a ~2% reward, booked as a reduction of net sales. [N483][N514][N440]
- Kirkland Signature carries higher margins than national brands, and management expects its share of sales to keep rising. [N501][N493]

## Revenue build-up
- Net sales = core merchandise + ancillary businesses (gasoline, pharmacy) + other businesses (e-commerce, travel). [N515]
- Gasoline was 11% of FY2026 sales. Volume rose 7%, which added sales separately from pump prices. [N485][N518]
- Digitally-enabled sales were 11% of sales, and their comparable sales grew 21%. [N486][N520]
- FY2026 comps came from both ticket and frequency. Q3 was mostly ticket. [N516][N469]
- Units: 939 warehouses versus 914. Paid members were 82.9M in Q3 versus 79.6M. [N490][N472]
- The U.S. and Canada made up 86% and 83% of net sales and operating income. Executive members were ~three-quarters of sales. [N497][N488]

## Core thesis
- Revenue rises if comps and openings (up to 33 planned for FY2027) continue. Fuel inflates reported growth: it added 221 bp to Q3 sales growth, after cutting ~64 bp in Q2. [N521][N471][N457]
- Margins are more likely to be squeezed: the average U.S. wage is ~$33, tariff refunds are expected to be mostly passed to members as lower prices, and the fee-increase tailwind is fading. [N496][N522][N475]
- Renewal is diluted by online and international sign-ups, which hurts membership income. [N524]
- Latest guidance is the 10-K of 2026-08-30, under three months old. The newest earnings-call notes are from March 2025, over six months old, so call commentary is stale. [N521]

## What changed over time
- 2021: Executive members were 42% of paid members but a little over 70% of sales, which made the Executive tier the core of the model. [N579]
- 2023: Costco discontinued charter shipping, with $391 million of charges for the year. [N257]
- 2024: The U.S. and Canada fee rose to $65 on September 1, worth about $370M of revenue over two years. [N305][N341]
- 2025: The wage floor rose to $20.00 in March, and year-end renewal slipped to 92.3% (U.S. and Canada) and 89.8% (worldwide). [N408][N796][N404]
- 2026: Openings guidance fell from 33 for the year (February) to about 29 (May). [N462][N476][N478]

## Catalysts
- The $4B buyback authorization runs through January 2027. [N194]
- The next top-of-scale wage increase is scheduled for March 2027. [N791]
- FY2027 openings are planned at up to 33, including five relocations. [N521]
- IEEPA tariff refunds received after FY2026 are expected to be mostly reinvested in lower prices, so the gross margin effect is undated. [N522]
- Extended producer responsibility fees are rising across multiple states, and the timing is undated. [N509]

## KPIs to track
- Comps split into ticket and frequency; gross margin (11.32% in Q1 FY2026). [N516][N445]
- Renewal rates (92.3% U.S. and Canada, 89.8% worldwide); paid members; Executive share of sales. [N489][N472][N488]
- Digitally-enabled comps and the warehouse count. [N520][N490]
- Gasoline price per gallon and gallons sold. [N518][N526]

## Potentially useful datasets
- Average retail gasoline price per gallon: tracks the fuel sales effect on reported net sales. Public pump prices are available before quarterly results. [N526][N481]
- Warehouse visit counts: track traffic, the frequency leg of comps. [N512]
- Costco app download counts: track digital member acquisition and e-commerce engagement. [N779][N768]
- USD/CAD rates: track the translation effect on Canadian operations. [N422]
- Commodity and gasoline price indices: proxy merchandise cost and margin pressure. [N511]
