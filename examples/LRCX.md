# LAM RESEARCH CORP (LRCX)

## Business model
- Sells wafer fabrication equipment and services to memory, foundry and IDM chipmakers; a customer support group sells services, spares and upgrades for the installed base. [N499][N500]
- Qualified tools tend to stay on a line, which creates switching stickiness. Systems cost up to tens of millions each, so a few recognitions can swing a quarter. [N519][N546]

## Revenue build-up
- Two lines: systems (new leading-edge equipment) and customer support-related (services, spares, upgrades, non-leading-edge Reliant). [N559][N501]
- Systems rose 29.5% ($3.39B) in FY26 on foundry spending. The September 2026 quarter was up $2.19B (62%) year over year. Foundry share gained 900 bp and memory lost 300 bp in FY26. [N560][N574][N562][N563]
- Customer support rose 20.2% ($1.40B) in FY26 on spares and non-leading-edge equipment; the September 2026 quarter was up 40% year over year. [N561][N575]
- Non-U.S. sales were about 93% of revenue; China was 34% in FY26, against 42% in FY24. A few large customers dominate. [N531][N548][N532]
- Management controls delivery timing, so recognition can shift between quarters. [N512]

## Core thesis
- Revenue follows customer capex. Management expected 2026 WFE growth from AI demand in both memory and non-memory. It rises if that spending holds and falls if export licensing expands. [N508][N533][N543]
- Gross margin (GM) is mix-driven. It reached 50.5% in FY26 and 52.3% in the September 2026 quarter on favorable customer mix. Steel and aluminum tariffs offset part of the gain. [N564][N577][N568]
- Stale guidance: the latest numeric guide is from the April 2025 call (June 2025 quarter revenue $5B ±$300M, GM 49.5%). The latest outlook statement is the March 2026 10-Q, over six months old. The notes hold nothing newer. [N857][N858][N508]

## What changed over time
- 2022: October export rules were estimated to cost $2B–$2.5B of 2023 revenue. [N197][N701]
- 2023: China hit a 48% revenue share in the September quarter, then 42% for FY24. [N770][N433]
- 2024: FY24 GM rose to 47.3% from 44.6% while R&D rose to 12.8% of revenue. [N372][N373]
- 2025: Foundry passed memory in the March quarter (48% vs 43%). April China restrictions removed another $700M of second-half plans. [N408][N409][N861]
- 2026: FY26 GM reached 50.5%. The September quarter revenue rose 22% sequentially, and Lam executed $1.00B of accelerated buybacks. [N564][N573][N581]

## Catalysts
- November 2026: China's partial rare-earth export-control suspension ends unless extended; the notes give no exact day. [N540]
- Undated: further Commerce restricted-list additions and license decisions on Chinese customers. [N443][N549]
- Undated: proposed PFAS restrictions. [N527]

## KPIs to track
- Systems vs support growth, and foundry/memory share. [N574][N575][N562]
- GM (52.3%) and tariff drag. [N577][N568]
- Deferred revenue ($2.66B) and customer down payments. [N578][N565]
- China share of revenue (34% in FY26). [N548]
- Effective tax rate (16.9%) and headcount (about 24,300). [N583][N579]

## Potentially useful datasets
- WFE spending by foundry and memory customers: it tracks systems demand, and customer spending precedes shipment recognition. [N570][N584]
- DRAM capex: it tracks the DRAM investment that drove the March 2026 quarter, ahead of recognition. [N514][N502]
- Memory fab utilization: it tracks spares and upgrade demand, which management called the first signal of memory recovery. [N823][N763]
- NAND layer-count conversion spending: it tracks the NAND upgrade driver, and upgrade orders precede shipments. [N862][N819]
- China equipment imports or fab tool orders: they track license-exposed China revenue before it is reported. [N780][N456]
