# HOME DEPOT, INC. (HD)

## Business model
- World's largest home improvement retailer by fiscal 2025 net sales, selling through stores and online, to consumers (DIY and DIFM) and Pros. [N495][N496]
- DIFM installation is delivered by third-party installers, with the company acting as general contractor. Proprietary products carry higher margins than national brands. [N509][N499]
- SRS, which now includes GMS, is a specialty distributor to trade pros, with almost all sales on open account. Trade credit carries default risk. [N503][N862][N525]

## Revenue build-up
- FY2025 net sales were $164.7B. SRS and GMS added about $6.3B. [N537][N538]
- Retail comps are ticket × transactions. Q3 FY26 comp was +2.2%: ticket +3.0%, transactions -0.5%. The nine-month comp was +1.5%. [N582][N583]
- GMS added about $3.3B in nine months, and Mingledorff's (acquired May 11, 2026) added to Q3 growth. [N584][N585]
- Online was 16.5% of Q3 sales, up 12.8%. A weaker dollar added $81M. [N586][N593]
- Q2 is seasonally the largest quarter. [N504]

## Core thesis
- Revenue: comps are ticket-led while traffic falls, as customers shift to smaller repair projects amid housing affordability pressure. Revenue growth beyond acquisitions needs transactions to turn positive, which depends on housing turnover and affordability improving. [N591][N583][N526][N527]
- Margins: gross margin is diluted by the SRS/GMS mix (Q1 33.0% vs 33.8%) and by fuel and input costs. SG&A rose to 19.1% of sales because costs outgrew comps. Operating margin needs comps to outgrow costs. [N565][N590][N564]
- Staleness: the latest comp, margin and EPS guidance in the notes is from February 2025 (FY2025: comps about +1%, adjusted operating margin about 13.4%), more than six months old. FY2026 guidance covers only capex of about $4B and no buybacks. [N869][N871][N588][N589]

## What changed over time
- 2021: transactions turned negative (Q2 -6.0%, Q3 -5.8%), so growth became price- and mix-led. [N71][N84]
- 2023: guidance was cut and the flat-comp target was missed (Q1 -4.5%, Q3 -3.1%), with lumber deflation and fewer transactions. [N737][N752][N758][N250]
- 2024: buybacks paused in March, and SRS closed June 18 for $18.25B, cutting gross margin. [N359][N374][N357][N860]
- 2025: GMS closed September 4. Comps shifted from traffic to ticket (Q3 ticket +1.8%, transactions -1.6%). [N489][N482]
- 2026: the Supreme Court struck down the IEEPA tariffs, and about $730M of refunds had been received by August 2, mostly as lower COGS. [N529][N576]

## Catalysts
- Fiscal 2026 year-end reporting: the buyback pause and debt-reduction priority run through fiscal 2026. [N589]
- Tariff litigation and possible new tariffs, undated, could change costs. [N578][N592]
- Fiscal 2027: completion of the roughly 80-store plan, then 15 to 20 new stores a year. [N505]

## KPIs to track
- Comp transactions and average ticket. [N582]
- Gross margin and SG&A as % of sales. [N565][N564]
- Online share and growth. [N586]
- Inventory turns (4.4x) and ROIC (25.7%). [N544][N545]
- SRS location count (over 1,340) and store count (2,372). [N572][N587]

## Potentially useful datasets
- Store foot traffic or card transaction counts: track comp transactions. They update far more often than the quarterly report. [N580][N566]
- Existing home sales and housing turnover: track project demand tied to home purchases, a driver of the traffic decline. [N532][N526]
- HELOC and cash-out refinance activity: track financing for large projects. It should lead big-ticket ticket and transactions. [N885]
- Tariff-category import and customs data: track imported-goods cost. It should lead gross margin. [N533]
- Severe-weather event data: track storm-driven product demand. [N522]
