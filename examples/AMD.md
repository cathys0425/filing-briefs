# ADVANCED MICRO DEVICES INC (AMD)

## Business model
- Fabless semiconductor company selling AI accelerators, server CPUs, GPUs, APUs, FPGAs and related data center, client, gaming and embedded products [N642][N592].
- Sales run on customer forecasts and purchase orders with no minimum commitments, and standard orders can be cancelled [N548][N565][N566]. Semi-custom adds non-recurring engineering fees plus product sales [N568].
- Hyperscalers buy a substantial share of data center products, and a few customers account for much of revenue [N552][N650].

## Revenue build-up
- Data Center: $8.5B in the September 2026 quarter, up 97%, on EPYC and Instinct GPUs [N643]. FY2025 was $16.6B [N606].
- Client is a units × ASP story. In the September quarter units rose 6% and ASP rose 1% [N644]. In the June quarter units rose 34% and ASP fell 6% on a mobile mix shift [N632][N633].
- Gaming fell 40% to $0.8B on lower semi-custom revenue [N645]. Embedded rose 29% to $1.1B [N646].
- Revenue is historically weighted to the second half [N647].

## Core thesis
- Latest dated guidance in the notes is the May 2025 call, about 18 months old, so it is stale: strong double-digit 2025 growth, second-half-weighted GPU revenue [N893]. Current direction rests on reported results, not guidance.
- Revenue rises if Data Center demand holds. Named drivers are the OpenAI 6 GW MI450 agreement and the Meta agreement of up to 6 GW [N605][N623], on an annual Instinct cadence [N556]. Concentration and customers' in-house chips could cut this [N560][N650].
- Margins rise with Data Center mix (54% vs 52%) [N648]. They fall if revenue slows against R&D up 33% [N640], $30.3B of purchase commitments [N639], or warrant dilution of up to 160M shares each for OpenAI and Meta [N637].

## What changed over time
- 2022: Xilinx closed Feb 14, adding FPGAs and embedded products [N177].
- 2023: The AI GPU ramp became the growth plan, with a Q4 Data Center GPU guide of about $400M and more than $2B for 2024 [N798].
- 2024: Data Center GPU guidance rose from $3.5B to above $5B [N829][N855]. Gaming and Embedded fell as customers drew down inventory [N858][N859].
- 2025: Export rules on MI308 cost about $800M and cut Q2 gross margin to 40% [N527][N533]. The OpenAI warrant was issued [N545].
- 2026: Data Center is the engine, and the Meta agreement and warrant add dilution [N643][N623][N624].

## Catalysts
- Taalas and World Labs acquisitions expected to close in fiscal Q4 2026, subject to regulatory approvals [N654].
- Seasonal second-half weighting shows in fiscal Q4 results [N647].
- Warrant tranche vesting is tied to GPU purchase milestones and AMD stock price targets, with no dates given [N625].
- Equity investment commitments are expected to be funded through fiscal 2028 [N655]. Up to $450M of contingent Sanmina cash is possible through 2028 [N613].

## KPIs to track
- Data Center revenue growth and GPU share [N643].
- Gross margin (54%) [N648].
- Client units and ASP [N644].
- Gaming semi-custom decline [N645].
- R&D growth [N640].
- Inventory (up $2.2B in FY2025) and purchase commitments [N617][N639].
- Guarantee exposure of up to $8.5B [N652].
- International mix (57%) [N649].

## Potentially useful datasets
- PC and notebook unit shipments: tracks Client units [N656][N641]. Shipments are sell-in to OEMs, so they should show the volume trend ahead of AMD's report.
- Data center AI GPU shipment or deployment counts: tracks Instinct demand [N564][N630]. Deployments precede recognized revenue.
- Utility interconnection and power-capacity announcements: track data center demand, because customer power and capacity build-outs gate their purchases [N589][N579].
- Chinese AI accelerator import data: tracks MI308 China sales, which depend on import rules and licensing [N581]. Licensing and import changes come before the revenue.
- Ryzen retail prices: track Client ASP [N547].
