# ANALOG DEVICES INC (ADI)

## Business model
- Sells mixed-signal integrated circuits across more than 75,000 SKUs. Data converters are the largest product family [N416][N412].
- Sells through a direct sales force, distributors, independent reps and a website. There are generally no long-term contracts, so visibility rests on orders and forecasts [N409][N410].
- Pricing is set at design-in and stays fairly fixed, sometimes for over a decade [N752].
- Hybrid manufacturing: internal fabs plus partners. TSMC and other foundries supply more than half of wafers [N768][N418].
- Policy is to return 100% of free cash flow [N731].

## Revenue build-up
- Industrial, the largest end market, was about 49% of Q3 FY26 revenue ($1.97B, +53%) [N484].
- Automotive was $998M (+16%), and its share fell from 30% to 25% [N485].
- Communications is led by AI data-center wireline demand [N486]. Consumer growth comes from portable products [N399].
- Distributors took 58% of Q3 FY26 revenue, up from 55%, as Industrial grew [N487]. Distributor revenue is recognized at sale, net of estimated price protection and stock rotation, so estimate errors can reverse it later [N425].
- Q1 is seasonally lowest because of customer plant shutdowns [N413].

## Core thesis
- Revenue follows Industrial demand and AI data-center wireline. Q1 to Q3 FY26 growth was 30%, 37% and 40% [N461][N472][N483]. Revenue falls if Industrial reverses, as in FY24 (revenue -23%, Industrial -35%) [N356][N355].
- Gross margin follows factory utilization and mix. Q3 FY26 was 67.3% vs 62.1% [N489]. Management said in Nov 2024 that 70% would likely need about $2.7B or more of quarterly revenue [N762]. Utilization normally runs 85–90% [N743].
- Cash conversion is weakening even as dollars rise. Operating cash flow was 43% of revenue vs 47% in the January quarter [N470], and 33% vs 38% for six months [N477]. Inventory days were 131 in Q3 [N490].
- Staleness: the latest quarterly revenue and EPS guidance in the notes is from Feb 2025 (Q2 FY25 revenue $2.5B ±$100M) [N773][N774], well over six months old. The newest guidance is capex of 4–6% of FY26 revenue (Aug 2026 10-Q) [N491].

## What changed over time
- 2021: Maxim closed Aug 26 [N57]. It drove about 70% of fiscal 2022 Q2 growth [N116].
- 2023: revenue growth slowed to 2% for the year. Industrial fell 19% sequentially in Q4, and inventory days climbed to 188 [N663][N680][N689].
- 2024: revenue fell 23% and gross margin fell 690 bp on lower utilization and mix [N356][N361]. Capex was reset to 4–6% of revenue for FY25 [N362].
- 2025: revenue rose 17% and gross margin rose 440 bp. Wireline Communications tied to AI and Industrial restocking drove growth [N452][N456][N453][N454].
- 2026: growth accelerated. Industrial regained the top share while Automotive slowed [N484][N485].

## Catalysts
- December 2026: about $0.9B of debt comes due, already classed as current [N468][N480].
- 2027: management's target for $1B of Maxim revenue synergies [N763][N692].
- Undated: the Penang, Malaysia facility is held for sale (as of the Nov 2025 10-K) [N420].

## KPIs to track
- Gross margin and factory utilization [N489][N743].
- Book-to-bill and 13-week backlog [N415][N414].
- Days cost of sales in inventory (131) and channel inventory vs the 7–8 week target [N490][N758].
- Distributor share of revenue (58%) [N487].
- Operating cash flow as a share of revenue [N470].
- Capex as a share of revenue (4–6%) [N491].

## Potentially useful datasets
- Hyperscale data-center capex and AI server shipments track Communications wireline demand. Build-outs precede equipment orders [N421][N493].
- Distributor sell-through and inventory data track the 58% channel. Management watches sell-through more closely than sell-in [N433][N617].
- Manufacturing PMI tracks Industrial demand, which management says is highly correlated with the core business [N735].
- China EV production tracks automotive content. Vehicle output precedes chip orders [N766].
- Gray-market chip prices track diversion from authorized channels, which can signal pricing stress [N340].
