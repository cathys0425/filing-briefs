# UNION PACIFIC CORP (UNP)

## Business model
- Freight revenue equals carloads times average revenue per car (ARC); ARC moves with price, traffic mix and fuel surcharges [N541]. 2025 freight revenue was $23.2B across Bulk, Industrial and Premium [N467].
- Customer incentives tied to lanes or cumulative volume reduce reported revenue [N542]. Fuel surcharges recover much of fuel cost with a lag of about two months [N487].

## Revenue build-up
- 2025 mix: Industrial about 37%, Bulk about 33%, Premium about 30% [N469][N468][N470].
- Bulk: Powder River Basin is the largest coal flow [N471]. Q1 2026 bulk revenue rose 10% on 12% volume growth from gas-driven coal burn [N515]. Coal then fell in Q3 on low gas prices and high plant inventories [N547].
- Premium: international intermodal followed West Coast imports, falling 28% in Q1 2026 after a strong 2025 [N516]. Q3 2026 intermodal volume rose 11% but carries lower ARC [N545]. Automotive is down year to date on weak vehicle demand [N552].
- Q3 2026 freight revenue rose 15% on fuel surcharges, 6% carload growth and core pricing, partly offset by intermodal-heavy mix [N544]. ARC rose 8% [N550].

## Core thesis
- Revenue: carload growth is intermodal-heavy, so mix dilutes ARC. Revenue growth needs core pricing and fuel surcharges to outrun that [N544][N545].
- Margins, fuel: record locomotive fuel prices pressure the operating ratio, which worsened to 59.7% in Q2 2026 [N551][N532]. Surcharges lag by up to two months, so revenue recovery shifts between quarters [N518][N497].
- Margins, productivity: Q1 2026 workforce productivity rose 7% and velocity 9% [N520][N519]. Pricing set to outpace inflation is accretive to the operating ratio [N1008].

## What changed over time
- 2022: guidance fell from a 55.5% operating ratio target to about 60% on network congestion [N679][N769][N786].
- 2023: the operating ratio worsened each quarter, reaching 63.4% in Q3. Schneider volume added negative mix [N283][N813].
- 2024: the operating ratio improved to 60.3% in Q3 and productivity rose 12%. Buybacks restarted in Q2 [N374][N373][N952].
- 2025: the Norfolk Southern merger agreement was signed July 28 [N481]. International intermodal was front-loaded, then fell sharply in the second half [N499]. The operating ratio was 59.8% [N501].
- 2026: the STB rejected the merger application as incomplete in January, and buybacks are paused [N482][N554]. Fuel drove the Q2 operating ratio to 59.7% [N532].

## Catalysts
- Merger outside date is January 28, 2028, and a $2.5B termination fee may be owed in certain cases [N481][N483].
- A revised STB filing would restart the completeness review; the date is not set [N482].
- The 2026 capital plan was raised to about $3.5B from about $3.3B [N553][N535].
- H.R.1, enacted July 4, 2025, made 100% bonus depreciation permanent [N463].
- STB reciprocal switching rulemaking could change rate and access conditions [N478].

## KPIs to track
- Carloads, ARC, and gross and revenue ton-miles; the gap reflects mix [N548].
- Fuel surcharge revenue and diesel price per gallon [N546][N537].
- Operating ratio [N532].
- Freight car velocity, terminal dwell and workforce productivity [N519][N520].
- International intermodal carloads [N516].
- Service performance index [N549].

## Potentially useful datasets
- West Coast port import container volumes track international intermodal carloads. Imports rose 33% in Q1 2025 alongside Premium growth. Port data is observable before quarterly carloads are reported [N555][N432].
- Weekly U.S. diesel prices track locomotive fuel cost and surcharge revenue. Surcharges lag fuel by about two months, so diesel moves lead reported revenue [N540][N488].
- Natural gas prices and power-sector coal burn track coal carloads, which fell on low gas in Q3 2026. Gas prices lead the carload response [N525][N547].
- Housing starts track lumber and forest products shipments, which are weak. Starts are published ahead of carloads [N390][N523].
