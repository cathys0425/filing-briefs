# Broadcom Inc. (AVGO)

## Business model
- Two segments: semiconductor solutions (including IP licensing) and infrastructure software (private cloud, mainframe, cybersecurity, enterprise software, FC SAN) [N541][N552].
- Manufacturing is outsourced, with TSMC making about 95% of contract-made wafers and no long-term capacity commitments [N485][N486][N492].
- Distributors and OEMs account for most semiconductor sales, and revenue is recognized on delivery to distributors [N514].
- VCF is sold as subscriptions. Software revenue timing depends on whether contracts carry a termination right [N476][N548].

## Revenue build-up
- Semiconductors: five end markets, with AI spanning custom XPUs, Ethernet switching, NICs, optics and XPU-based racks [N472][N473]. August-quarter segment revenue was $20,839M, up 127%, on custom AI accelerators and AI networking [N553][N554].
- Products revenue includes upfront license revenue: $1,755M, $1,964M and $3,465M in the Feb, May and Aug 2026 quarters, which distorts comparisons [N537][N549][N562].
- Software growth is led by VCF, including license revenue on non-terminable contracts [N555].
- Channel: distributors were 48% of FY25 revenue, and one distributor was 42% of revenue in the May quarter [N470][N545]. Top five end customers were about 55% of quarterly revenue [N557]. China was 17% of FY25 [N522].

## Core thesis
- Staleness: the newest document is the 10-Q dated 2026-08-02. The latest quantitative guidance in the notes is from the 2025-03-06 call, over 20 months old [N814][N815]. Treat quarterly numbers as unguided.
- Revenue rises only if hyperscaler demand for custom XPUs and AI networking keeps being deployed. The three-customer SAM was sized at $60–90B for fiscal 2027, and the AI XPV platform targets more than 20 GW through 2028 [N806][N559]. Order timing makes quarters lumpy [N547].
- Gross margin is pressured by lower-margin semiconductor mix and by rack sales, which can raise operating margin while lowering gross margin [N564][N490]. Gross margin was 69% in the August quarter [N563].
- Tax and credit are the downside risks: the global minimum tax is expected to raise tax rates materially, and the AI-rack backstop liability is about $29B [N500][N560].

## What changed over time
- 2023: the VMware deal closed on November 22, 2023, funded with $30.4B of term loans [N261][N267].
- 2024: gross margin fell to 63% from 69% on VMware amortization [N415]. AI revenue was $12.2B [N800].
- 2025: fiscal semiconductor revenue was $36,858M, up 22%. Tax incentives cut the provision by $2,709M, and a $1,321M CAMT valuation allowance was booked [N518][N501][N528].
- 2026: semiconductor revenue grew 127% to $20,839M [N553]. Concentration rose and backstop exposure appeared [N557][N560].

## Catalysts
- Share repurchase program authorized in March 2026 runs through December 31, 2026 [N538].
- FY2026 results, for the year ending November 1, 2026, will show the global minimum tax and higher capex [N500][N524].
- Fiscal 2027: the GILTI deduction falls to 37.5%, and the fiscal 2027 SAM is sized at $60–90B [N193][N806].
- Through 2028: the AI XPV platform targets more than 20 GW [N559].
- Fiscal 2028: the Malaysia tax holiday expires [N298].

## Potentially useful datasets
- AI accelerator and AI networking shipments: tracks the main growth driver, and inventory was built ahead of expected shipments [N529][N550].
- Hyperscaler capex and announced data-center power or capacity: tracks XPU and networking demand, and customer spending precedes Broadcom deliveries [N798][N811].
- Distributor sell-through and inventory: tracks end demand behind distributor-channel sales [N530].
- TSMC monthly revenue or wafer data: tracks wafer supply from the main foundry, which precedes shipments [N286][N492].
